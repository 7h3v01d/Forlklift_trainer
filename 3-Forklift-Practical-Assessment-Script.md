# Forklift practical assessment script

Assessors want to hear you name each check, say what you are looking for, and say what you would do if it fails. Talk through everything out loud, slowly, and point at each item. Built from your Study Guide and Learner Guide. Where a phrase goes beyond the booklets, such as what a good fork or chain check looks like or looking over both shoulders at start, it is standard practice that assessors normally expect.

## 1. Before you start

Plan out loud before you touch the forklift. Use the job instructions or task sheet you are given.

| Do this | Say this |
| --- | --- |
| Talk to the right people | "Before I start I would speak to my supervisor, co-workers, managers and first aid officers about known hazards and controls." |
| Check the weather | "I have checked the forecast, so I can plan the work and any risk controls." |
| Work out the load | "The load weighs X kg. I got that from the labels, weighbridge certificate, by calculating it or by weighing it." |
| Show your calculation | "Items first, then item weight, then the pallet last." Write it down. |
| Check capacity | "The data plate says my forklift lifts Y kg at a 600 mm load centre. My load is under that and its load centre is within it." |
| Choose the forklift and attachment | "A gas forklift is for outside. An electric forklift is for confined areas with limited air flow. I need no attachment, or I need a [attachment] and its capacity is reduced." |
| Plan the route | "My route is [name it]. It avoids slopes, wet patches, blind corners, people and overhead lines." |
| Scan for hazards | Look up, around you and down. Say: "Overhead there are no power lines. At eye level I can see pedestrians, other traffic and blind corners. On the ground I can see the surface condition and any slopes." |
| Check communications and permits | "Communications are safe and adequate, I know the hand signals, and no permits are needed for this task." |
| Put on PPE | "I am wearing hi-vis, non-slip boots and my hard hat where required." |

If you see a hazard, name the control: "There is a pedestrian walkway crossing my route, so I will slow down, sound the horn and give way."

## 2. Pre-start walk-around (engine off)

Walk a full circle around the forklift, then check from the ground and the seat. Touch or point to each item.

| Item | Say this | Look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Log book and manuals | "I am checking the log book for earlier faults and that they were fixed, and the operator's manual is with the forklift." | Open faults, missing manual |
| Data plate | "The data plate is secured and readable, and shows the rated capacity." | Unreadable or missing plate (tag out) |
| Under the forklift | "No oil, fuel or hydraulic leaks under it." | Puddles, drips |
| Fluid levels | "Hydraulic oil, engine oil, transmission oil, brake fluid and cooling water are all at the right level." | Low levels, leaks |
| Fuel or power | "LPG cylinder is secure, valve works, no leaks. (Electric) Battery is secured, charged and the terminals are clean." | Loose cylinder, damaged battery |
| Tyres and wheels | "Tyres have no large pieces of rubber missing, no flats, even tread, not worn to the wear line, and the wheels are secure. Air-filled tyres are at the same pressure." | Missing rubber, uneven wear, loose wheel nuts |
| Forks | "Forks are straight, no cracks, no wear at the heels, and the fork locking pins are in place." | Cracks, bent forks, missing pins |
| Mast, chains, pulleys, carriage | "Mast is undamaged, chains are even with no rust or stiff links, pulleys and carriage are secure." | Damage, dry or stretched chains |
| Hydraulics | "Hydraulic rams and hoses show no splits, bulges or leaks." | Weeping rams, bulging hoses |
| Guards | "The overhead guard protects me from falling objects. The load backrest stops the load hitting the mast and protects me. Both are undamaged and secure." | Cracks, bent bars, loose bolts |
| Structure | "No visible damage, paint separation or stressed welds." | Cracks, fresh welds |
| Attachments (if fitted) | "The attachment is securely fixed to the manufacturer's specifications, and I have allowed for its reduced capacity." | Loose fitting, no manual |
| Mirrors, seat and seat belt | "Mirrors are clean and correctly positioned, the seat is secure, and the seat belt latches and locks." | Frayed belt, cracked mirrors |
| Lights and signage | "Flashing amber light, head, brake and reverse lights are fitted and undamaged. Decals and signs are readable." | Broken lenses, missing labels |

**Fault found?** Say it as you do it: "I am removing the key, tagging the forklift out of service, recording it in the log book and reporting it to my supervisor." Do not use the forklift until it is repaired.

## 3. Start-up operational checks (ignition ON)

1. Use 3 points of contact to climb on. Say: "Three points of contact."
2. Sit, adjust the seat and mirrors, fasten the seat belt. Say: "Seat belt on. It stops me falling out if the forklift tips."
3. Check you are in neutral, hand brake on, forks lowered, then start it as the manufacturer says. Listen for abnormal noises.
4. Do each test with the area clear of people, cranes and other vehicles, and say what you are testing and what a pass looks like.

| Test | Say this |
| --- | --- |
| Steering | "Steering turns smoothly through full lock with no play." |
| Foot brake | "Foot brake holds firmly and does not sink." |
| Hand brake | "Hand brake holds the forklift." |
| Horn | "Horn works." Sound it. |
| Lights | "Head, brake and reverse lights and the flashing amber light all work." |
| Reverse beeper | "Reverse alarm sounds in reverse." |
| Hydraulic levers | "Lift, lower, tilt forward and back, and side shift (and any attachment function) all move smoothly to their full extent." |
| Gauges | "Gauges read normal: fuel or battery level, temperature and oil pressure, with no warning lights." |
| Safety devices | "Deadman's switch and emergency descent device work, where fitted." |
| Leaks after moving | "No leaks after moving a few metres." |

End with: "All operational checks are complete and the forklift is safe to operate." If anything fails, stop and report it before you move any load.

## 4. Moving loads

**The five steps.** Say them as you start: "Check the load, check the route is safe, pick it up, drive safely, place it."

**Picking up**

1. Approach front on, square to the load. "I am checking the load is stable and the pallet is not damaged."
2. "The heaviest part is against the load backrest. The load weight and load centre are within the data plate."
3. Insert the forks all the way, under both sides. Say: "I am never lifting on one fork."
4. Raise to axle height, tilt the load back slightly, and check the side shift is centred.
5. Look over both shoulders before you move. Check mirrors.

**Travelling**

- Say: "Load at axle height or as low as possible, mast tilted back."
- Slow speed, sound the horn at blind corners and intersections, keep to forklift zones, give way to pedestrians.
- "I do not travel or turn with the load raised, because it can tip the forklift or lose the load."
- Turning: "Rear end swing is about three times the forklift's speed. I keep to the inside of the turn and watch behind me."
- If the load blocks your view: "I will travel in reverse, use a spotter, or repack if it is safe." Then look over both shoulders and check mirrors.
- Wet surface: "I slow down, brake gently, turn slowly and avoid ramps."
- Power lines: "I stay clear of overhead lines. Tiger tails show where they are but do not insulate."

**Ramps**

- "On a ramp I travel straight up or down and do not turn or cross it. The load faces uphill."

**Stacking and racking**

- "Flat level surface, heaviest goods at the bottom, and a stable stack."
- Approach slowly, raise to clear, lower onto the stack, tilt the forks slightly forward, look over both shoulders and reverse out without scraping the pallet.
- Never raise or lower a load over people.

**Loading docks and containers**

- "Dock plates or bridge plates must be in place and secured before I cross the gap. The flashing light shows the truck is safe to load."
- Use an electric forklift inside a container or cool room, never an internal combustion forklift.

**Attachments**

- "I have checked the attachment's manual and load chart. Capacity is reduced." Rotate loads only when stopped. For a jib: low speed, low load, jib vertical or tilted back, treat the forklift as partly loaded.

## 5. Shut down and post-check

**Park.** Say: "I am parking on a flat surface, out of the way of traffic. Not near first aid stations, fire extinguishers, emergency exits, doorways or walkways, and not on a ramp."

1. Tilt the forks forward and lower them to the ground.
2. Select neutral and apply the hand brake.
3. Turn the engine off and remove the key. Say: "Key removed to prevent unauthorised use."
4. Turn off the LPG cylinder valve (gas forklift).
5. Place the battery on charge in a ventilated area, away from naked flames, with no smoking (electric forklift).

**Post-operational check.** Walk round again and say what you are checking.

| Check | Say this |
| --- | --- |
| Forklift | "Visual inspection of the forklift for anything odd, wrong, broken or damaged." |
| Environment | "No fluid leaking on the ground under it." |
| Signals | "No alarms, lights or electronic warnings showing something is wrong." |
| Gauges | "Temperatures and levels of fuel, oil and other fluids are normal." |
| Security | "The forklift is secured and the key is removed." |

**Report.** Complete the log book or checklist: "I am recording the hours, the checks done, and any fault with the make and model, the type of fault and my name." If you found a defect: stop, remove the key, tag out of service, record it, report it to an authorised person.

**Refuelling note.** Always switch the engine off first, because fuel, vapours or gases can ignite.

## 6. If something goes wrong

| Situation | What to say and do |
| --- | --- |
| Fault found in a check | "Removing the key, tagging it out of service, recording it, reporting it to my supervisor." Do not use it. |
| Brakes, steering or hydraulics fail | "Stopping the forklift, tagging it out of service and reporting to my supervisor." |
| Load drops suddenly or a hydraulic problem | "Lowering the load and turning the forklift off." Then check for damage and hydraulic lines for splits or bulges, and report it. |
| Forklift starts to tip sideways | "Stay in the forklift, brace myself until it is stationary and safe to exit, lean away from the tipping side, and never jump." |
| Contact with overhead lines | In order: "1. Warn people to stay away. 2. Try to break contact. 3. Stay in if safe, otherwise jump or shuffle away with feet together to at least 8 metres, without touching the forklift and ground at once. 4. Report to my supervisor, the power company and the safety regulator. 5. Do not use the forklift until it has been checked." |
| Unsure of line voltage | "I would call the local power company and ask the electrical authority before working near it." |
| Unclear signal from a co-worker | "Stopping and asking them to repeat the signal." |
| Workplace emergency | "Alert the right people, say what the emergency is, tell workers which areas are unsafe, give information to emergency services, and give way to emergency vehicles." |

## 7. Questions the assessor may ask

Keep answers short and use the booklet words.

| Question | Model answer |
| --- | --- |
| What is a hazard, and what is a risk? | A hazard may cause harm or injury. A risk is what could happen to you when exposed to the hazard. |
| What is your duty of care? | Look after my own safety and other people's safety, follow instructions and workplace rules, and report problems. |
| What is the order of the hierarchy of controls? | Elimination, substitution, isolation, engineering, administrative, then PPE, which is the least effective. |
| Where do you find the rated capacity? | On the data plate. |
| What is load centre distance? | From the heel of the forks to the load's centre of gravity. Most forklifts are rated at 600 mm, half a 1200 mm pallet. |
| How do you work out a load centre? | Length (or width) divided by two. A 1400 mm load gives 700 mm, over a 600 mm rating. |
| Can the forklift lift this load? | Check the weight against the rated capacity AND the load centres against the plate. If either is over, no. |
| What is the fulcrum? | The balance point, under the front tyres where they touch the ground. |
| What is rear end swing? | The rear of the forklift swinging wide in a turn, because it steers on the back wheels. About three times the forklift's speed (the Learner Guide says up to three and a half). |
| What are the stability triangle and centre of gravity? | The centre of gravity must stay within the triangle between the drive wheels and the centre of the rear axle, or the forklift can tip. |
| What can tip a forklift sideways? | Turning too fast, turning on a ramp, turning with the load raised, uneven surfaces, an uneven load, one fork, uneven or flat tyres, side shift not centred. |
| What can tip it forwards? | Overloading, severe braking, load too high, driving too fast, load facing forwards down a hill, uneven surface, too much forward tilt, load not against the heels. |
| Why centralise the side shift? | The load's centre of gravity would be off to one side and the forklift would be unstable. |
| What do the guards do? | The overhead guard protects my head from falling objects. The load guard (backrest) protects the mast and me from the load. |
| Why wear the seat belt? | It stops me falling out in an accident and being thrown into the structure. |
| What gas does a charging battery give off? | Flammable (explosive) gas, so charge in a ventilated area away from flames. |
| What do tiger tails do? | Show where power lines are. They do not insulate. |
| Minimum distance from power lines in Queensland? | Guidance: 3.0 m up to 132 kV, 6.0 m up to 330 kV, 8.0 m above. Confirm with the electrical authority, because Queensland also depends on operator competency. |
| When can a forklift carry a passenger? | Only if it is designed and equipped with a seat for more than one person. |
| What does an attachment do to capacity? | Reduces it. Read the attachment's manual. |

## 8. Common mistakes and last check

**Mistakes that cost marks**

- Skipping the pre-start or doing it silently. Say every item.
- Not tagging out a forklift with a fault, or driving off with a fault found.
- Lifting on one fork, or forks not all the way in.
- Travelling or turning with the load raised.
- Not looking over both shoulders before reversing.
- Not centring the side shift.
- Turning on a ramp, or the load not facing uphill.
- Forgetting to show your weight calculation.
- Not removing the key and not tilting the forks forward at shutdown.
- Getting on or off without three points of contact, or not wearing the seat belt.

- [ ] I know the tip-over and power line steps.
