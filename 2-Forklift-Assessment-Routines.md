# Forklift (LF) assessment routines – Queensland

Follow four phases in this order and say what you are checking out loud as you do it: pre-check, start-up operational checks, safe operating, then shut down. Everything below comes from your two Just Careers booklets (Study Guide version 2.1 and Learner Guide version 3.1), plus a few standard-practice items marked as such.

## Phase 1 – Pre-check (engine OFF)

Do all of this before you turn the forklift on.

**A. Plan the job**

1. Talk to supervisors, co-workers, first aid officers, fire wardens and truck drivers about known hazards and controls.
2. Check the weather forecast so you can schedule the work and plan risk controls.
3. Work out the load weight: weighbridge documents, calculating it, labels on the load, weighing it, or the inventory system.
4. Confirm the load location, the safest route, the type of forklift (gas or electric) and any attachment needed.
5. Confirm the forklift's capacity covers the load weight AND load centre (read the data plate).
6. Confirm communications are safe and adequate, any permits are in place, and you have the equipment the task needs.
7. Scan for hazards: power lines, people, other forklifts, truck traffic, loading docks, blind corners, slopes, wet or uneven surfaces, poor lighting, poor ventilation, damaged racking, dangerous goods.

**B. Inspect the forklift (walk around, say each item aloud)**

| Area | What you check |
| --- | --- |
| Paperwork | Log book, operator's / manufacturer's manual, data plate readable |
| Tyres and wheels | Large pieces of rubber missing, flat, unsecure wheels, underinflated, uneven tread, worn down; all air-filled tyres at the same pressure |
| Fluids and power | Fluid levels, battery, LPG cylinder (gas forklift) |
| Lifting gear | Forks, fork locking pins, mast, carriage, chains, pulleys, hydraulic rams, hydraulic hoses |
| Guards | Overhead guard (protects your head from falling objects) and load guard (protects the mast and operator from the load) |
| Attachments | Secure and correct for the task; check the attachment's manual; remember reduced capacity |
| Operator area | Mirrors, seat, seat belt, structural damage |

**C. If you find a fault** (for example an unreadable data plate): remove the key, tag it "out of service", report to your supervisor.

## Phase 2 – Start-up operational checks (ignition ON)

Sit in the seat, put your seat belt on, turn the ignition on, then test each item and call it out:

1. Steering
2. Brakes
3. Horn
4. Lights
5. Hydraulic levers (lift, tilt, side shift, any attachment function)
6. Gauges
7. Reverse beeper

If any of these fail: stop, remove the key, tag "out of service" and report to your supervisor. Do not operate the forklift.

## Phase 3 – Safe operating routine

**Picking up and travelling**

1. Check the load weight and load centre against the data plate before the forks go in.
2. Load sits against the heel of the forks, forks evenly spaced and under both sides of the load (standard practice). Never carry a load on one fork arm.
3. Centralise the side shift before travelling.
4. Travel with the load at a safe height: at axle height or as low as possible.
5. If the load blocks your view: travel in reverse, use a spotter, or repack the load if it is safe to do so.
6. Keep the speed down, brake gently and turn slowly, especially on wet or slippery surfaces; keep to site speed limits (the guide's sign example is 5 km/h).
7. Watch rear end swing: the rear swings round at about three times the speed of the forklift, so keep the front wheel close to where you are turning.
8. Use the horn and warning devices, and stop and ask for a repeat if a co-worker's signal is unclear.

**Never**

- Turn on a ramp, turn with a raised load, or turn too fast (side tip-over).
- Drive overloaded, brake hard, or drive too fast or with the load too high (forwards tip-over).
- Drive with an uneven or unbalanced load, over uneven surfaces, or with uneven tyres.
- Raise, lower or carry a load over people.
- Carry passengers (only allowed if the forklift is designed for it).

**Ramps and surfaces**

- The load must face UP the ramp, and you do not turn on the ramp.
- Avoid ramps and slopes when wet; avoid soft soil, potholes, trench covers, railway tracks and cracked surfaces.

**Stacking**

1. Flat level surface.
2. Heaviest goods on the bottom.
3. Keep the stack stable.

**Loading docks and containers**

- Place dock plates or bridge plates across the gap between the truck and the dock before shifting a load.
- Watch the flashing light or signal that shows the truck is safe to load or unload.

**Power lines**

- Look up before you lift. Stay outside the Queensland exclusion distances (see numbers below) and clarify with the electrical authority if unsure of the voltage.

**Attachments**

- Read the attachment's manual, remember capacity is REDUCED, and with a jib expect a swinging load and lower capacity.

## Phase 4 – Shut down and post-check

**Park in a safe spot.** Never park near first aid stations, emergency exits or fire extinguishers, or on ramps or walkways.

1. Tilt the forks forward and lower them to the ground.
2. Hand brake on.
3. Neutral gear.
4. Turn the engine or ignition off.
5. Remove the key (prevents unauthorised use).
6. Turn off the LPG cylinder (gas forklift).
7. Place the battery on charge in a ventilated area (electric forklift).

**Post-check walk-around (standard practice):** look over tyres, forks, mast, hoses and guards for new damage or leaks, and note any fault in the log book.

**If you find a fault:** tag "out of service", remove the key, report to your supervisor.

**Refuelling:** turn the engine off first. A running engine risks igniting fuel, vapours or gases.

## Emergencies

| Situation | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Forklift tipping sideways | Stay in the forklift (never jump out, you could be crushed), brace yourself, lean away from the tipping side |
| Contact with overhead power lines | In order: 1) warn people to stay away, 2) try to break away from the lines, 3) stay in the forklift if safe, otherwise exit and jump or shuffle 8 m away with feet together, 4) report to a supervisor, 5) check the forklift before reuse |
| Major failure of brakes, steering or hydraulics | Stop the forklift and tag it "out of service", then report to a supervisor |
| Unsure of power line voltage | Call the local power company |
| Unclear signal from a co-worker | Stop the forklift and ask them to repeat the signal |

## Terminology to use

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Hazard | Something that may cause harm or injury |
| Risk | What could happen to you when exposed to a hazard |
| Duty of care | Take care of your own safety and others' safety |
| HRWL / Class LF | High risk work licence; the forklift class. The regulator can suspend, cancel or refuse to renew it |
| Control measures | Signs, barriers, walkways, flashing lights, traffic mirrors, lighting |
| Data plate | Shows rated capacity, load centre and lift height |
| Rated capacity | Overall weight the forklift can lift safely at the stated load centre |
| Load centre distance | From the heel (vertical face) of the forks to the load's centre of gravity |
| Fulcrum | The balance point, under the front tyres touching the ground |
| Heel of the forks | Vertical face of the forks; the load sits against it |
| Dim B / Dim C | Horizontal and vertical load centres on some data plates (length ÷ 2, height ÷ 2) |
| Tare | Weight of an empty container |
| Rear end swing | Rear of the forklift swinging wide in a turn |
| Side shift | Moves the load guard left and right; keep it centralised when travelling |
| Reduced capacity | Lower lifting capacity when an attachment is fitted |
| Spotter | Person who guides you when your view is blocked |
| Dock plate / bridge plate | Plate laid across the gap between truck and loading dock |
| Out of service tag | Tag fitted to a faulty forklift, with the key removed |
| JSA / SWP / SDS / SWL chart | Job safety analysis, safe work procedure, safety data sheet, safe working load chart |

## Numbers and calculations

**Queensland power line distances** (guidance from the guide; clarify with the electrical authority before work starts):

| Line voltage | Minimum distance |
| --- | --- |
| Up to and including 132 kV | 3.0 m |
| Above 132 kV up to and including 330 kV | 6.0 m |
| Above 330 kV | 8.0 m |

**Load weight:** work out total items first, then item weight, then add the pallet last, and always show your calculations.

- Cement: 5 bags × 8 layers = 40 bags; 40 × 25 kg = 1000 kg; 1000 + 30 kg pallet = 1030 kg.
- Drums: 6 × 150 kg = 900 kg; 900 + 35 kg pallet = 935 kg.
- Loaded container: 5000 kg tare + 7000 kg contents = 12000 kg.

**Load centre:** width (or length) ÷ 2. Most forklifts in Australia are rated at 600 mm (half a 1200 mm pallet).

- A 1400 mm load gives 700 mm (over a 600 mm rating).
- A 1000 mm load gives 500 mm (within a 600 mm rating).
- A 1300 mm load gives 650 mm (over a 600 mm rating).

**Can the forklift lift it?** Check BOTH the weight against rated capacity AND the load centres against the data plate. If either is over, the answer is NO. Capacity also changes with mast vertical versus forward tilt, and falls when the load is not against the heels or an attachment is fitted.

**Other figures:** rear end swing is about three times forklift speed; exclusion jump distance after power line contact is 8 m.

## Extra points from the Learner Guide

These come from your second booklet (TLILIC0003 Learner Guide, version 3.1) and slot into the phases above.

**Phase 1 and 2 additions**

- Check the logbook for earlier faults and make sure they were fixed. Use a forklift inspection checklist (Appendix A and B of the guide).
- Look under the forklift for oil leaks, check the load backrest, and on solid tyres check for missing rubber, even wear and the wear limit line.
- Engine checks: hydraulic oil, engine oil, transmission oil, brake fluid, cooling water, fuel and battery.
- Use 3 points of contact getting in and out, wear your seat belt, and listen for abnormal noises at start-up.
- Test every movement and function to its full extent, including the deadman's switch and emergency descent device where fitted.

**Reporting a fault, in order:** stop and remove the keys; tag "out of service"; record it in the logbook or checklist; report it to your supervisor or an authorised person. Do not use the forklift until it is repaired.

**Shifting a load:** check the load, make sure the work area is safe, pick up, drive safely, place.

1. Pick up: approach front on, insert the forks all the way, raise to axle height and tilt back slightly, travel at axle height or as low as possible.
2. Place: approach slowly, raise to clear the stack, lower the load, tilt the forks slightly forward, look over both shoulders and reverse out without scraping the pallet.

**Phase 4 additions**

- Park on a flat surface out of the way. Do not park on slopes, near doorways, next to train tracks (no closer than 2 m) or under power lines with too little clearance. On an incline, chock the front wheels as well.
- Post-operational checks: look over the forklift and the ground (fluid leaks), check signals (alarms and lights) and gauges, then secure it with the keys removed.
- Charge batteries in a well-ventilated area away from any naked flame, and do not smoke near a charging battery.
- A fault report form needs the make and model, site identification numbers, the type of fault and who reported it.

**Emergencies**

- Rollover: remain in the forklift, brace until it is stationary and safe to exit, and do not try to jump out.
- Power line contact: after the five steps, report to your supervisor, the power company and the safety regulator, and do not reuse the forklift until it has been checked and returned to service.
- Load drops suddenly or a hydraulic problem: lower the load, turn the forklift off, check hydraulic lines for splits or bulges, and report it.
- Any workplace emergency: alert the right people, say what the emergency is, tell workers which areas are unsafe, give information to emergency services, and give way to emergency vehicles.

**Risk language**

- Hierarchy of controls, best to worst: elimination, substitution, isolation, engineering controls, administrative controls, PPE.
- Risk level comes from consequence and likelihood. An Extreme risk means the task must not proceed.
- Tiger tails show where power lines are but do not insulate them, so exclusion zones still apply.

**Where the two booklets differ**

- Rear end swing: the Study Guide says about 3 times the forklift's speed, the Learner Guide says up to 3.5 times. Know both.
- Checking load weight: the Learner Guide lists four methods (weigh, markings, calculate, weighbridge certificate). The Study Guide adds the inventory system.
- Parking order: the Study Guide lists forks, hand brake, neutral, keys, LPG, battery on charge. The Learner Guide lists forks, neutral and hand brake, engine off and keys, LPG, then site-specific steps.

## Confirm with your assessor

- Battery gas: say "flammable gas" (it is explosive, so keep the battery in a ventilated area on charge). Your guide names it hydrogen sulphide, and a charging lead-acid battery actually gives off hydrogen, so only use a specific name if your assessor asks for it.
- Regulator: the guide lists "Worksafe Qld"; in Queensland this is Workplace Health and Safety Queensland, so confirm the name your assessor expects.
- Power line distances depend on voltage and operator competency in Queensland, so quote the guide's figures as guidance and say you would check with the electrical authority.
- Items marked standard practice (forks fully under the load, post-check walk-around, log book entry) are not in the guide but assessors normally look for them.
- Your workplace may use its own pre-start checklist; follow that form's order if it differs from this one.
